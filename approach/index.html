Approach

A narrow intervention built to produce proof.

The first engagement focuses on one workflow, uses evidence rather than assumption and moves from diagnosis to operating controls that can be tested.

Diagnose

Establish the intended process, the actual process, failure patterns and their commercial consequences.

Redesign

Specify the workflow, ownership, evidence, controls, indicators and escalation logic required.

Implement

Deploy the practical assets, configure suitable tools and transfer the process into daily work.

Verify

Test use, data quality, control completion and exceptions; correct weaknesses before handover.

Control standard

Every finding must be operationally testable.

A credible finding is tied to a specific failure point, source evidence, responsible owner, impact and verification method.

Evidence

Documents, system records, interviews, samples and observed workflow behaviour—not vague frustration alone.

Ownership

A named role with clear authority, deadline and escalation route—not accountability assigned to “the team”.

Verification

A defined test showing whether the control is used and whether the relevant failure is reducing.

Buying-risk discipline

Fixed scope. Fixed timeline. Fixed price.

The initial scope remains narrow enough to approve and concrete enough to create a usable decision or operating pack.

✓

Founder-led

The person diagnosing the problem remains accountable for the design and implementation.

✓

Tool-neutral

Existing systems are used first. New technology is introduced only if it materially supports the required controls.

✓

Implementation-oriented

Recommendations are translated into working routines, assets and configured workflows where included in scope.

✓

Measured

Relevant indicators and a baseline are agreed so improvement can be assessed without inflated promises.

Working requirements

What Zevalios needs from the client

Executive sponsorship

A sponsor who can authorise access, resolve ownership questions and support implementation across functional boundaries.

Relevant evidence

Sample contracts, process records, trackers, reports, invoices, system extracts or other evidence sufficient to understand actual workflow behaviour.

Access to operators

Short, focused interviews with the people who sell, hand over, deliver, approve, record and invoice the work.

An internal owner

A named person who can coordinate information, validate the design and sustain the process after handover.
